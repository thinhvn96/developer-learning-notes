The saga pattern: distributed work without distributed transactions

System Design · Lesson 0009 · step 9 of 11 · Advanced · ~12 minutes · Home · System Design ladder · Roadmap

Today's win: explain a saga, choreography versus orchestration, and compensating transactions, and run a small orchestrator.

Idea

A saga maintains data consistency across services by "coordinating a sequence of local transactions". Each local transaction commits in its own service and triggers the next step through an event or message; if a step fails, compensating transactions undo the completed steps. It suits microservices that each own a database, where traditional ACID and two-phase commit are a poor fit.[1]

Vocabulary

  • Compensable transactions can be undone; the pivot transaction is the point of no return; retryable transactions after it are idempotent and must eventually succeed.[1]
  • Choreography: services exchange events with no central controller (simple, but hard to follow as it grows; risk of cyclic dependencies).
  • Orchestration: a central orchestrator tells participants what to do and handles compensation (better for complex flows; a point of failure).[1]
java system-design/examples/SagaDemo.java
--- payment fails
  do create order / do reserve stock / do charge payment
  FAILED: card declined -> compensating
  compensate reserve stock  (stock released)
  compensate create order   (order cancelled)

Source: SagaDemo.java (an in-memory orchestrator; real ones persist saga state). Cautions from the docs: sagas lack isolation (lost updates, dirty reads, fuzzy reads), compensations can fail, and steps must be idempotent; countermeasures include semantic locks, commutative updates, re-reading values, and versioning.[1] Related: outbox for reliable events, local transactions in Spring.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. A saga is...?

It coordinates local transactions with compensating transactions.[1]

2. Central controller style?

An orchestrator handles all transactions and tells participants what to do.[1]

3. Compensation on failure runs...?

Compensating transactions undo the changes of completed steps.[1]

4. Missing from sagas?

There is no built-in isolation across services.[1]
