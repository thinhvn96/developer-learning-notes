Sharding, shard keys and consistent hashing

System Design · Lesson 0010 · step 11 of 11 · Advanced · ~12 minutes · Home · System Design ladder · Roadmap · Tiếng Việt

Today's win: explain shard keys and the three main strategies, and show why hash-mod-N is painful when you add a shard.

Idea

"Divide a data store into a set of horizontal partitions or shards": each shard has the same schema and its own subset of the data. The shard key is the most critical decision; changing it typically means migrating all data. A good key is immutable, has high cardinality, spreads data and load evenly and matches the dominant queries; avoid monotonically increasing values and low-cardinality attributes.[1]

Strategies

  • Lookup (directory): a map from key to shard; flexible, extra lookup cost.
  • Range: good for range queries; hotspots and hard rebalancing.
  • Hash: even distribution; with plain hash(key) mod N, "adding or removing a shard reassigns most keys"; consistent hashing moves only a small fraction.[1]
  • Geographic: for residency and latency; risk of imbalance.[1]
java system-design/examples/ShardingDemo.java     # 100,000 keys, 4 -> 5 shards
hash(key) mod N            : 80.1% of keys move
consistent hashing (ring)  : 18.2% of keys move (ideal 20%)

Source: ShardingDemo.java. Costs the docs list: cross-shard queries and transactions, operational burden (monitoring, backups, schema changes). Consider read replicas, caching, or table partitioning before sharding.[1] Related: Kafka partitions are sharding by key (lesson) and Elasticsearch shards (lesson).
