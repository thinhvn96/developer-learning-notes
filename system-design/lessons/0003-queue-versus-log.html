Queue versus log: choosing RabbitMQ or Kafka

Today's win: pick RabbitMQ or Kafka for a requirement and defend the choice with documented behavior.

Two different models

RabbitMQKafka
ShapePublishers send to exchanges, which route copies to queues by bindings; consumers take from queues[2]Events are appended to partitions of a topic; consumers read at an offset[1][4]
After consumptionConsumers acknowledge deliveries; un-acked messages are requeued if the consumer dies[3]"Events are not deleted after consumption"; retention is configured per topic[1]
OrderingQueue-level delivery to consumers (see the RabbitMQ docs for the exact guarantees under concurrency)Per partition; same key goes to the same partition[1]
PositionBroker tracks acks per delivery[3]Consumer position is "a single integer, the offset of the next message to consume", so consumers can rewind and reprocess[4]
RoutingRich: direct, fanout, topic, headers exchanges[2]By topic and partition key
Scaling consumersAdd competing consumers on a queueUp to one consumer per partition in a group[4]
Failure handlingDead letter exchanges, delivery limits on quorum queuesConsumer decides: commit order gives at-most or at-least once[4]

Rules of thumb (my synthesis of the rows above)

  • Choose RabbitMQ for task queues and flexible routing where each message is a unit of work to be handled once and then gone, with per-message retry and dead-lettering.
  • Choose Kafka when the stream itself is the asset: many independent consumers, replay after a bug fix, high throughput, per-key ordering, event sourcing.
  • Both give at-least-once in the common configuration, so make consumers idempotent regardless ([3], [4]).
The "rules of thumb" are interpretation, not quotes. In an interview, name the documented behavior first (retention versus ack-and-remove, offsets versus acks), then your conclusion. Throughput claims need your own benchmark; I have not cited any here.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. Which system keeps events after they are read?

Events are not deleted after consumption in Kafka; retention is per topic.[1]

2. Where do you get rich routing rules?

Exchanges route copies to queues via bindings and several exchange types.[2]

3. Replay after a consumer bug fix is easiest with?

Kafka consumers can rewind because position is just an offset.[4]

4. Both deliver at least once commonly. Consumers need?

Redelivery is expected in both, so repeated processing must be safe.[3]
