Retries, backoff and idempotency

System Design · Lesson 0002 · step 2 of 11 · Core · ~10 minutes · Home · System Design ladder · Roadmap · Tiếng Việt

Today's win: explain when a retry helps, when it makes an outage worse, and why idempotency is a precondition.

When to retry

Transient faults (momentary network loss, a busy service) "are typically self-correcting", so retrying after a suitable delay is likely to succeed. The pattern lists three strategies: cancel if the fault is not transient, retry immediately if it is rare, and retry after a delay for common busy or connectivity failures.[1]

How to retry safely

  • Space out the retries: increase the delay incrementally or exponentially, up to a maximum number of attempts, and choose delays "to spread requests from multiple instances of the application as evenly as possible".[1] Randomizing the delay (jitter) is how the example below achieves that spreading; the term itself is my label, the goal is from the docs.
  • Do not hammer: "An aggressive retry policy with minimal delay between attempts, and a large number of retries, could further degrade a busy service".[1] After many failures, stop sending requests and probe occasionally: the Circuit Breaker pattern.[1]
  • Idempotency: "If so, it's inherently safe to retry. Otherwise, retries could cause the operation to be executed more than once." A server may process a request but fail to respond.[1]
  • One layer only: nested retry policies multiply delays; fail fast at lower levels.[1]

Not for: long-lasting faults, business-logic errors, or as a substitute for scaling a service that keeps reporting busy.[1]

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. Fault is not transient. Best strategy?

If retrying is unlikely to succeed, cancel and report the exception.[1]

2. Why space retries with growing delays?

Aggressive retries can further degrade a busy service.[1]

3. Which operations are inherently safe to retry?

If the operation is idempotent it is inherently safe to retry.[1]

4. After many failures, which pattern helps?

Stop requests to the failing resource and probe occasionally, as in Circuit Breaker.[1]
