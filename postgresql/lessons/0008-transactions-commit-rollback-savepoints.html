Transactions: COMMIT, ROLLBACK and savepoints

PostgreSQL · Lesson 0008 · step 4 of 13 · Foundation · ~10 minutes · Home · PostgreSQL ladder · Roadmap · Tiếng Việt

Today's win: wrap steps in a transaction, predict what a failure does, and use a savepoint for partial rollback.

All or nothing

A transaction "bundles multiple steps into a single, all-or-nothing operation". Intermediate states are not visible to other transactions, and if a failure prevents completion, none of the steps affect the database.[1] Use BEGIN ... COMMIT, or ROLLBACK to cancel. Without BEGIN, each statement is its own transaction.[1]

Savepoints

Savepoints "allow you to selectively discard parts of a transaction while committing the rest": SAVEPOINT s, then ROLLBACK TO s. The savepoint remains defined after rolling back to it.[1]

./run.sh 08-transactions.sql
BEGIN; UPDATE Alice -30; UPDATE Bob +30; COMMIT;        -> Alice 70, Bob 80
BEGIN; ...second UPDATE violates CHECK...; COMMIT;
   ERROR: new row for relation "accounts" violates check constraint
   (the COMMIT was answered with ROLLBACK)               -> balances unchanged
SAVEPOINT s1; ...; ROLLBACK TO s1; ...; COMMIT;          -> Alice 60, Bob 80, Wally 10

Note the middle case: after an error inside a transaction block, PostgreSQL aborts the whole transaction; a later COMMIT ends up as a rollback (observed in the run). Source: 08-transactions.sql. This is what Spring's @Transactional automates (lesson), and NESTED propagation maps to savepoints.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. A transaction is...?

It bundles steps into an all-or-nothing operation.[1]

2. A statement outside BEGIN is...?

Every statement is implicitly wrapped in a transaction.[1]

3. ROLLBACK TO s1 does what?

It discards only what happened after the savepoint.[1]

4. After ROLLBACK TO, is s1 still defined?

A savepoint continues to be defined after rolling back to it.[1]
