Ingest pipelines: parse inside Elasticsearch

ELK Stack · Lesson 0012 · step 12 of 14 · Advanced · ~12 minutes · Home · ELK Stack ladder · Roadmap · Tiếng Việt

Today's win: do light parsing inside Elasticsearch with an ingest pipeline, test it with simulate, and know its limits versus Logstash.

What they are

Ingest pipelines "let you perform common transformations on your data before indexing", such as removing fields, extracting text values and enriching data. A pipeline is a series of processors that run sequentially; then the document is indexed. Use one with the pipeline query parameter, or as the index's index.default_pipeline; test with the simulate API.[1]

Limits: pipelines work one document at a time as it is indexed, so they cannot split documents, aggregate across documents or modify already-indexed data (the enrich processor gives limited lookup).[1]

PUT _ingest/pipeline/access   {grok, lowercase, set}
simulate "10.0.0.7 GET /orders/42 200"
  -> {client: 10.0.0.7, method: get, path: /orders/42, status: 200, pipeline: access}
simulate "garbage"
  -> Provided Grok expressions do not match field value: [garbage]
PUT weblogs/_doc/1?pipeline=access   then   q=status:200  -> found

The same grok syntax %{SYNTAX:SEMANTIC} works here as in Logstash.[2] Choosing between them is a design trade-off: an ingest pipeline needs no extra service; Logstash offers more inputs/outputs and buffering (Logstash lesson); the docs pages I read do not give a comparison, so state your reasoning.
