Strategy

Design Patterns · Lesson 0023 · step 23 of 30 · Core · ~10 minutes · Home · Design Patterns ladder · Roadmap

Today's win: define interchangeable algorithms behind one interface, swap them at runtime, and see when a lambda is enough.

Intent

Strategy is "a behavioral design pattern that lets you define a family of algorithms, put each of them into separate classes, and make their objects interchangeable." Use it to switch algorithm variants at runtime, isolate business logic from algorithm details, and replace big conditionals.[1]

Example

java design-patterns/examples/StrategyDemo.java
by length     : [fig, pear, kiwi, banana]
reverse alpha : [pear, kiwi, fig, banana]
no discount   : 200
10% off       : 180
lambda -25    : 175

Comparator is the JDK's best-known strategy: "a comparison function, which imposes a total ordering", passed to sort methods "to allow precise control over the sort order", with factory and combinator methods like comparing, thenComparing and reversed.[2] The Cart swaps pricing strategies at runtime, including a lambda. Source: StrategyDemo.java. This is the Open/Closed lesson (OOP) as a pattern.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Swap algorithms at runtime
  • Isolates implementation details
  • Composition instead of inheritance
  • Open/Closed

Cons

  • Needless complexity for simple, stable algorithms
  • Clients must know the differences to choose
  • Functional languages may make it unnecessary

(From the pattern's page.)[1] Strategy vs State: both use composition, but states may know each other; Strategy vs Template Method: composition (runtime) versus inheritance (static); Strategy vs Decorator: internals versus outward behavior.
