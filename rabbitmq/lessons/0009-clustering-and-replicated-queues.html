Clustering: nodes, odd numbers and replicated queues

RabbitMQ · Lesson 0009 · step 9 of 9 · Senior · ~12 minutes · Home · RabbitMQ ladder · Roadmap

Today's win: explain what a cluster shares, why queue contents are not replicated by default, why odd node counts matter, and prove a quorum queue survives losing its leader node.

What a cluster is

"A logical grouping of one or more (three, five, seven, or more) nodes, each sharing users, virtual hosts, queues, streams, exchanges, bindings, runtime parameters and other distributed state." All state is replicated across nodes "an exception to this are message queues, which by default reside on one node, though they are visible and reachable from all nodes."[1] To replicate queue contents use a replicated type such as quorum queues.[1]

Odd numbers

"Odd numbers of cluster nodes are highly recommended", and two-node clusters are "highly recommended against" since nodes cannot identify a majority after connectivity loss. Quorum queues need consensus among members.[1] Quorum queues are built on Raft and are the default choice for replicated, highly available queues.[2]

Try a real cluster

cd rabbitmq/examples && ./03-cluster-quorum.sh     # 3 containers: rmq1, rmq2, rmq3
Running Nodes: rabbit@rmq1 rabbit@rmq2 rabbit@rmq3
q.classic  classic  leader rmq1  members [rmq1]                    messages 3
q.quorum   quorum   leader rmq1  members [rmq1, rmq2, rmq3]        messages 3
docker stop rmq1                     # the node hosting both queues' leaders
asked via rmq2:  q.classic  rabbit_classic_queue  (no message count: unavailable)
                 q.quorum   quorum  3               (still available, 3 messages)

The classic queue lived on one node and became unavailable when that node stopped; the quorum queue kept its 3 messages on the surviving members. Source: 03-cluster-quorum.sh. Network partitions and split-brain handling go beyond the pages read; the clustering guide covers them. Kafka's equivalent is replication with ISR: replication lesson.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. Where does a classic queue live by default?

Message queues by default reside on one node.[1]

2. Recommended node counts?

Odd numbers are highly recommended.[1]

3. Why avoid two-node clusters?

Two nodes cannot identify a majority after connectivity loss.[1]

4. What kept the queue alive in my run?

The quorum queue had replicas on all three nodes.[2]
