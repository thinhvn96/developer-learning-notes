Consumer acknowledgements, prefetch and redelivery

RabbitMQ · Lesson 0003 · step 3 of 9 · Core · ~10 minutes · Home · RabbitMQ ladder · Roadmap

Today's win: explain manual acknowledgements, what happens when a consumer dies, why redelivery forces idempotent consumers, and how prefetch limits in-flight messages.

Acknowledgements

Deliveries are identified by delivery tags. A consumer answers with basic.ack (positive), basic.nack (negative, a RabbitMQ extension) or basic.reject (negative, more limited than nack). With multiple=true, RabbitMQ acknowledges all outstanding tags up to and including the given one.[1]

If a consumer disconnects without acking, "any delivery (message) that was not acked is automatically requeued", with the redeliver flag set, so consumers "be implemented with idempotence in mind."[1] The AMQP overview says the same: the broker redelivers to another consumer.[2]

Automatic ack is a trap

Automatic acknowledgement "should be considered unsafe and not suitable for all workloads", and consumers can be overwhelmed by deliveries and build a backlog in memory.[1] Use manual acks.

Prefetch

Channel prefetch limits unacknowledged messages in flight; 0 means no limit, and "values in the 100 through 300 range usually offer optimal throughput."[1] Lower prefetch gives fairer distribution among slow workers; higher improves throughput.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. Consumer dies with unacked messages. Result?

Un-acked deliveries are automatically requeued.[1]

2. Why is auto-ack considered unsafe?

The docs call automatic acknowledgement unsafe for many workloads and warn about backlog buildup.[1]

3. Prefetch of 0 means what?

A value of 0 means no limit on unacknowledged messages.[1]

4. A redelivered message requires the consumer to be...?

Consumers must be implemented with idempotence in mind.[1]
