Publisher confirms and durability

RabbitMQ · Lesson 0004 · step 4 of 9 · Core · ~10 minutes · Home · RabbitMQ ladder · Roadmap

Today's win: list what you must set for a message to survive a broker restart, and what publisher confirms tell (and do not tell) a producer.

Durability needs two things

Queues and messages can be durable (persisted to disk) or transient (memory). To avoid data loss "both the queue and messages must be marked durable."[2] The example declares "durable":true queues.

Publisher confirms

Consumer acks protect the consumer side. For the producer side, publisher confirms let the broker acknowledge receipt: once enabled, "the broker then confirms messages as it handles them by sending a basic.ack on the same channel." It may send basic.nack instead, meaning it "was unable to process the messages and refuses responsibility for them." Confirms are asynchronous, so applications should process them as a stream rather than waiting synchronously for each.[1]

Putting it together

  1. Durable queue + persistent messages.
  2. Publisher confirms on, and retry or alert on nack and on unroutable messages.
  3. Manual consumer acks, idempotent processing.

For replicated queues so a single node failure does not lose the queue, see the quorum queues lesson. Compare Kafka's approach in Kafka replication.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. To avoid loss on restart, what must be durable?

Both the queue and messages must be marked durable.[2]

2. How are publisher confirms delivered?

Publisher acknowledgements are emitted asynchronously on the same channel.[1]

3. A broker sends basic.nack to a publisher. Meaning?

A nack indicates the broker was unable to process the message and refuses responsibility.[1]

4. Do consumer acks confirm publishing?

Publisher confirms are the producer-side mechanism; consumer acks concern deliveries.[1]
