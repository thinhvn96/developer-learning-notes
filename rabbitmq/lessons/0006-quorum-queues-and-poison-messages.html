Quorum queues and poison messages

RabbitMQ · Lesson 0006 · step 7 of 9 · Advanced · ~12 minutes · Home · RabbitMQ ladder · Roadmap · Tiếng Việt

Today's win: explain why quorum queues are the default choice for replicated queues, and how the delivery limit turns poison messages into dead letters.

What they are

"The RabbitMQ quorum queue is a modern queue type which implements a durable, replicated queue based on the Raft consensus algorithm."[1] They suit long-lived queues with critical data (sales orders, for instance), and are not meant for temporary queues.[1] The docs' advice: "quorum queues should be considered the default choice when needing a replicated, highly available queue." Classic queue mirroring "was removed starting with RabbitMQ 4.0."[1]

Poison messages

A poison message is one that always fails processing and would be requeued forever. Quorum queues count redeliveries in the x-delivery-count header; since RabbitMQ 4.0 they apply a default delivery limit of 20. Past the limit the message is discarded, or dead-lettered if a DLX is configured.[1] Delivery-limit exhaustion is one of the four dead-letter reasons.[2]

At-least-once dead lettering

Quorum queues support traditional and at-least-once dead lettering, where "the source quorum queue will retain the dead-lettered messages until they have been acknowledged" by the dead-letter target.[1]

Interview pattern: quorum queue + manual acks + delivery limit + DLX, plus a job that inspects and replays the dead-letter queue. That is the standard answer to "how do you handle poison messages?".
