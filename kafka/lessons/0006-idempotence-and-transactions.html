Idempotent producers and transactions

Kafka · Lesson 0006 · step 16 of 23 · Senior · ~12 minutes · Home · Kafka ladder · Roadmap · Tiếng Việt

Today's win: explain what the idempotent producer and transactions guarantee, and what they do not.

Idempotent producer

Since 0.11.0.0 the producer supports an idempotent option "which guarantees that resending will not result in duplicate entries."[1] In the 4.3 configuration docs enable.idempotence is on by default, ensuring "exactly one copy of each message is written in the stream."[2] It removes duplicates caused by the producer's own retries.

Transactions

Producers can "send messages atomically to multiple topic partitions using transactions, so that either all messages are successfully written or none of them are."[1]

What this buys and what it does not

  • Producer retries no longer duplicate records in the log (idempotence).
  • A consume-transform-produce step can write its outputs atomically (transactions).
  • It does not make your side effects exactly-once: an email sent or a row written in another database is outside Kafka's guarantee. Keep consumers idempotent (see the delivery semantics lesson).
The design page's full description of exactly-once (and of consumers reading only committed transactional data) goes beyond the excerpts used here. Ask your teacher to walk through it, or read the "Message Delivery Semantics" section of the Design chapter yourself.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. What does the idempotent producer prevent?

Resending will not result in duplicate entries.[1]

2. Transactions let a producer write to many partitions...?

Either all messages are written or none.[1]

3. Is enable.idempotence on by default in 4.3 docs?

The docs list the default as true.[2]

4. Does Kafka make an email side effect exactly-once?

Guarantees cover writes into Kafka; external side effects still need idempotent handling.
