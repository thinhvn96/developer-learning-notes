Producer throughput: batching and compression

Kafka · Lesson 0014 · step 11 of 23 · Advanced · ~12 minutes · Home · Kafka ladder · Roadmap · Tiếng Việt

Today's win: tune linger.ms, batch.size and compression.type for throughput, explain why Kafka is fast (batching, page cache, zero-copy), and read a benchmark.

Why Kafka is fast

  • Batching: "Batching is one of the big drivers of efficiency"; the producer is willing to "trade off a small amount of additional latency for better throughput".[1]
  • Batch compression: "Efficient compression requires compressing multiple messages together rather than compressing each message individually." The batch stays compressed in the log and on the way to consumers.[1]
  • Page cache and zero-copy: "data is copied into pagecache exactly once and reused on each consumption", and sendfile lets the OS "send the data from pagecache to the network directly".[1] TLS disables this zero-copy path, because data must be encrypted in user space.

The knobs

  • linger.ms (default 5, was 0 before 4.0): wait a little so records join a batch; a full batch.size is sent immediately anyway.[2]
  • batch.size (default 16384 bytes): per-partition batch buffer.[2]
  • compression.type (default none; gzip, snappy, lz4, zstd): applied to whole batches, so better batching means better compression.[2]
  • buffer.memory (32 MB): when it is full, "additional send calls will block" up to max.block.ms, then fail.[3] This is the producer's backpressure.

Benchmark: one laptop, one broker

06-batching-and-compression.sh sends 500,000 small JSON records with kafka-producer-perf-test, acks=all:

linger.ms=0  batch.size=16384  compression.type=none  247892 records/sec (20.39 MB/sec) | on disk: 47036 KB
linger.ms=5  batch.size=16384  compression.type=none  351864 records/sec (28.94 MB/sec) | on disk: 47036 KB
linger.ms=20 batch.size=131072 compression.type=none  616522 records/sec (50.71 MB/sec) | on disk: 47016 KB
linger.ms=20 batch.size=131072 compression.type=lz4   510725 records/sec (42.01 MB/sec) | on disk:  5812 KB
linger.ms=20 batch.size=131072 compression.type=zstd  486854 records/sec (40.04 MB/sec) | on disk:  3528 KB

Reading it: bigger batches gave 2.5× the throughput. Compression cost some CPU on a single local broker (no real network), but cut disk use 8 to 13×; across a real network and with replication factor 3, fewer bytes usually wins. Your numbers will differ; the shape is what matters.

Interview answer: "For throughput: raise linger.ms and batch.size, turn on lz4 or zstd, keep idempotence and acks=all. For lowest latency: small linger, accept smaller batches. Always measure with perf-test on production-like data."

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. What does compression act on in Kafka?

Compression applies to full batches, so better batching improves the ratio.[2]

2. Default linger.ms in Kafka 4.x?

The default changed from 0 to 5 in Apache Kafka 4.0.[2]

3. buffer.memory is full. What does send() do?

Send calls block up to max.block.ms and then return a failed future.[3]

4. Which feature lets brokers skip user-space copies?

sendfile sends data from pagecache to the network directly.[1]
