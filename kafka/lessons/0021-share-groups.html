Share groups: queues for Kafka

Kafka · Lesson 0021 · step 21 of 23 · Senior · ~13 minutes · Home · Kafka ladder · Roadmap · Tiếng Việt

Today's win: explain share groups (Queues for Kafka, KIP-932), when to use one instead of a consumer group, and how acknowledgements, locks and delivery limits work.

The limit they remove

In a consumer group a partition belongs to exactly one consumer, so a topic with 4 partitions can never have more than 4 busy workers, and one slow record blocks its partition. Share groups let "a pool of share consumers ... cooperate on the work of consuming and processing records", "permitting multiple consumers to consume from the same partitions".[1] They became production-ready in Kafka 4.2.[3]

How it works

  • Records are delivered with a time-limited acquisition lock (default 30 s, share.record.lock.duration.ms). "While a record is acquired, it is not available for another consumer"; if the lock expires it is released.[1][2]
  • Per record the consumer answers ACCEPT (done), RELEASE (try again later, maybe elsewhere), REJECT ("unprocessable", not redelivered) or RENEW (still working, extend the lock).[1]
  • Each record gets at most share.delivery.count.limit attempts (default 5), so a poison pill cannot loop forever.[2]
  • The trade-off: "more flexible sharing of records than a consumer group, at the expense of record ordering."[1]
  • A new share group starts at the latest offset (share.auto.offset.reset).[2]
props.setProperty("share.acknowledgement.mode", "explicit");
KafkaShareConsumer<String, String> consumer = new KafkaShareConsumer<>(props);
consumer.subscribe(List.of("jobs"));
while (true) {
    for (var r : consumer.poll(Duration.ofMillis(1000))) {
        try { handle(r); consumer.acknowledge(r, AcknowledgeType.ACCEPT); }
        catch (Exception e) { consumer.acknowledge(r, AcknowledgeType.RELEASE); }
    }
    consumer.commitSync();
}

Run it: two workers, one partition

$ ./08-share-group.sh
consumer A: 8 records: job-8 job-9 job-10 job-11 job-16 job-17 job-18 job-19
consumer B: 12 records: job-1 job-2 job-3 job-4 job-5 job-6 job-7 job-12 job-13 job-14 job-15 job-20

The topic has one partition, and both consumers did work. With a consumer group one of them would have been idle. Note how ordering across the two workers is gone.

When to choose which

  • Consumer group: per-key order matters (account balance, order state machine), or you need replay by offset with exactly-once in Kafka.
  • Share group: independent jobs (send email, resize image), workers that scale beyond the partition count, per-record retry. This is the job you would otherwise reach for RabbitMQ for; the data still lives in a Kafka log with retention.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. Share group, one partition, three consumers. Busy?

Share groups let multiple consumers consume from the same partitions.[1]

2. Which ack means 'never redeliver this record'?

REJECT marks the record unprocessable and it is not redelivered.[1]

3. Default delivery attempt limit per record?

share.delivery.count.limit defaults to 5.[2]

4. What do share groups give up?

Flexible sharing comes at the expense of record ordering.[1]
