SOLID 5: Dependency Inversion Principle

OOP (Object-Oriented Programming) · Lesson 0021 · step 21 of 22 · Advanced · ~10 minutes · Home · OOP (Object-Oriented Programming) ladder · Roadmap · Tiếng Việt

Today's win: invert a dependency so high-level policy depends on an abstraction it owns, and wire concrete classes at the edge.

The principle

"One should depend upon abstractions, not concretes."[1] High-level modules (policy) should not depend on low-level modules (details); both depend on abstractions.

java oop/examples/DipDemo.java
email -> ALERT: disk full
sms   -> ALERT: disk full
[test double] ALERT: disk full

AlertService depends on the Notifier interface, which lives with the high-level policy. Email, SMS and a test double are details plugged in at the edge (main). Compare the tight service in coupling and cohesion. Source: DipDemo.java.

DIP, DI and IoC

Dependency inversion is the design principle; dependency injection is a technique for supplying the abstraction; an IoC container (Spring) automates the wiring: "objects define their dependencies only through constructor arguments ... The IoC container then injects those dependencies" (Spring IoC lesson).
