SOLID 1: Single Responsibility Principle

OOP (Object-Oriented Programming) · Lesson 0017 · step 17 of 22 · Advanced · ~10 minutes · Home · OOP (Object-Oriented Programming) ladder · Roadmap · Tiếng Việt

Today's win: state SRP as 'one reason to change' and split a class along its reasons to change.

The principle

SOLID comes from Robert C. Martin's 2000 paper "Design Principles and Design Patterns"; Michael Feathers coined the acronym. The Single Responsibility Principle: "There should never be more than one reason for a class to change."[1]

Applied

An invoice class that calculates tax, formats text and saves to storage has three reasons to change (tax rules, layout, storage). Split it:

java oop/examples/SrpDemo.java
INVOICE amount=200
saved invoice 200
tax: 20

TaxCalculator, InvoicePrinter and InvoiceStore each change for exactly one reason. Source: SrpDemo.java. Signals you are violating it: a class name ending in Manager or Util, many unrelated imports, tests that need heavy setup. Do not over-split: a "responsibility" is a reason to change, not one method per class. The anti-pattern at the other end is the God object.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. SRP in one phrase?

There should never be more than one reason for a class to change.[1]

2. Who introduced SOLID?

Martin's 2000 paper; Feathers coined the acronym.[1]

3. The invoice class had how many reasons to change?

Tax, layout and storage.

4. A symptom of an SRP violation?

Broad names and unrelated imports are typical signs.
