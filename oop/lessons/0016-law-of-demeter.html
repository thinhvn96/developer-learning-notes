The Law of Demeter

OOP (Object-Oriented Programming) · Lesson 0016 · step 16 of 22 · Advanced · ~10 minutes · Home · OOP (Object-Oriented Programming) ladder · Roadmap

Today's win: apply the Law of Demeter: replace a chain through an object's internals with a 'tell, don't ask' method.

The law

Also called the principle of least knowledge: "Each unit should only talk to its friends; don't talk to strangers." A method m of object a may call methods only on: a itself, m's parameters, objects created within m, a's direct attributes, and accessible globals. It discourages "reaching through" one object to another's internals.[1]

java oop/examples/LawOfDemeter.java
after reaching through: 70
after c.pay(20)       : 50

c.getWallet().setMoney(c.getWallet().getMoney() - 30) makes the caller depend on the fact that a customer has a wallet with money: change that structure and every caller breaks. c.pay(20) says what you want; the customer decides how. Source: LawOfDemeter.java. Caveat: the law is a guideline, not a ban on fluent APIs like builders or streams (my note).
