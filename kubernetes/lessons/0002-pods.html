Pods: the smallest deployable unit

Kubernetes · Lesson 0002 · step 2 of 15 · Foundation · ~10 minutes · Home · Kubernetes ladder · Roadmap

Today's win: explain what a Pod is, why Pods are disposable, and why you rarely create them directly.

Definition

"Pods are the smallest deployable units of computing that you can create and manage in Kubernetes." A Pod "is a group of one or more containers, with shared storage and network resources, and a specification for how to run the containers. A Pod's contents are always co-located and co-scheduled, and run in a shared context."[1] Most Pods hold a single container; multi-container Pods are for tightly coupled helpers.[1]

Disposable

Pods are "relatively ephemeral, disposable entities". A Pod stays on its node until it finishes, is deleted, is evicted for lack of resources, or its node fails.[1] Nothing restarts a lost bare Pod on another node.

So use a workload resource

"You rarely create Pods directly." Use a Deployment (replicated apps), a Job (batch), or a StatefulSet (stateful apps).[1] Here is the docs' example, saved as pod.yaml:

apiVersion: v1
kind: Pod
metadata:
  name: nginx
spec:
  containers:
  - name: nginx
    image: nginx:1.14.2
    ports:
    - containerPort: 80

Verified: I applied the manifests in examples/ to a throwaway kind cluster (Kubernetes v1.37.0, kubectl v1.37.1) and quote outputs from that run. The kind setup itself is not covered by the pages I read; follow the project's own docs.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. Smallest deployable unit in Kubernetes?

Pods are the smallest deployable units.[1]

2. Containers in one Pod share what?

A Pod has shared storage and network resources.[1]

3. Why not create bare Pods for production?

Pods are disposable; use a Deployment, Job or StatefulSet instead.[1]

4. Most common Pod layout?

Single-container Pods are the most common pattern.[1]
