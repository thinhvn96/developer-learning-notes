Services: a stable address for changing Pods

Kubernetes · Lesson 0004 · step 5 of 15 · Core · ~10 minutes · Home · Kubernetes ladder · Roadmap

Today's win: explain why Services exist, choose between ClusterIP, NodePort and LoadBalancer, and write port versus targetPort correctly.

The problem

Pods are ephemeral; they are created and destroyed and their IP addresses change. A Service is "an abstraction that exposes a set of Pods over a network" and gives clients a stable endpoint.[1] It finds its Pods with a label selector.[1]

apiVersion: v1
kind: Service
metadata:
  name: nginx-service
spec:
  selector:
    app: nginx
  ports:
    - protocol: TCP
      port: 80
      targetPort: 80

port is the port the Service exposes; targetPort is the container port (equal by default).[1] File: service.yaml.

Types

TypeExposure[1]
ClusterIP (default)only inside the cluster
NodePorteach node's IP at a static port
LoadBalancerexternally, via a cloud provider's load balancer
ExternalNamemaps the Service to an external DNS name

Kubernetes keeps EndpointSlices up to date as matching Pods come and go, so your application needs no changes.[1] The relationship to the previous lesson: the Service selector app: nginx matches the Pod template labels from the Deployment.

Retrieval practice

Answer from memory. One attempt each.

1. Default Service type?

ClusterIP exposes the Service only within the cluster and is the default.[1]

2. Which Service field is the container's port?

targetPort is the container port; port is what the Service exposes.[1]

3. How does a Service find its Pods?

Services use labels and selectors to identify backend Pods.[1]

4. Why not use Pod IPs directly?

Pods are ephemeral and their IPs change over time.[1]
